# RPG Content

## RPG-BG-DIRT-001 — Contract Spacer

Status: **STORY_CANON**

### Narrative meaning

The player grew up around Dirt Belt freight work rather than entering space as a social blank slate. Their background is not a chosen-one origin; it represents practical familiarity with cargo schedules, repair debt, dock labor, small-ship margins, militia checkpoints, and the informal trust networks that keep marginal trade routes alive.

The background can be implemented either as a selectable origin in a future RPG-start system or as a derived trait granted by an appropriate start package.

### Origin frame

A Contract Spacer may have been:

- a child or junior partner in a one-ship family business;
- a freight clerk at a station such as Hydra Station;
- a mechanic who took passage as partial payment;
- a cooperative dispatcher who eventually acquired a ship;
- a former deckhand whose captain defaulted on wages.

The background intentionally permits several personal histories so it can be reused across character generation.

### Starting knowledge

A Contract Spacer understands:

- which cargoes are time-sensitive even when their listed price is not exceptional;
- how captains hide maintenance problems until inspection;
- why small merchants sometimes accept bad contracts to preserve cash flow;
- how station warehouses, repair queues, and docking delays can matter as much as nominal commodity prices;
- the difference between a pirate threat, an insurance scam, and an ordinary late freighter;
- the social importance of reputation among repeat trading partners.

### Gameplay opportunities

When supporting systems exist, this background should unlock or modify:

- additional freight-contract dialogue;
- better estimates of whether a shortage is temporary or structural;
- alternate negotiation options with independent captains, station workers, and cooperative insurers;
- lower information cost for local trade rumors;
- special mission solutions involving cargo pooling, subcontracting, salvage rights, or deferred payment;
- occasional access to low-margin emergency work that is not advertised publicly;
- recognition from named Belt Mutual NPCs.

It should not grant a universal trade-price bonus. The advantage is information, relationships, and specialized options rather than passive wealth generation.

### Dialogue consequences

Possible tags for future dialogue conditions:

- `background: contract spacer`
- `knowledge: dirt belt freight`
- `contact: belt mutual`

Example use cases:

- identifying that a warehouse 'shortage' is actually a dispatch bottleneck;
- persuading a captain to accept partial payment because the player knows local freight norms;
- noticing that a repair invoice has been inflated;
- understanding why a cooperative cannot abandon one unprofitable route without collapsing trust elsewhere.

### Faction consequences

- Republic officials may treat the player as economically literate but not politically important.
- Independent merchants may open up sooner if the player demonstrates knowledge rather than wealth.
- Pirates may assume the player knows which cargoes or captains are vulnerable.
- Large corporate or Syndicate operators may see the background as useful field experience or as evidence of provincial habits, depending on character and context.

### Mission consequences

This background is designed to interact with dynamic stories generated by shortages, freight disruption, piracy, repair scarcity, and local credit crises.

It can create noncombat resolutions such as:

- pooling cargo across several small ships;
- accepting salvage instead of credits;
- renegotiating delivery sequence;
- identifying a fraudulent distress claim;
- arranging a temporary convoy through existing local contacts.

### Progression opportunities

The background can evolve into later traits or reputations, for example:

- `Freight Broker` — recognized for assembling reliable cargo chains;
- `Mutual Organizer` — trusted by several small carriers;
- `Route Shark` — feared for exploiting local distress;
- `Convoy Hand` — known for protecting vulnerable traffic;
- `Dockside Fixer` — able to connect crews, mechanics, buyers, and unofficial labor.

Progression should depend on behavior, not a simple experience threshold.

### World-state inputs

Future support may consume:

- current system;
- commodity shortage severity;
- local freight demand;
- piracy pressure;
- recent convoy losses;
- repair capacity;
- local unemployment or migration pressure;
- player reputation with local merchants and governments;
- prior Contract Spacer story outcomes.

### World-state outputs

Possible outputs include:

- strengthened or weakened local freight capacity;
- named merchant survival or bankruptcy;
- changed shortage duration;
- changed pirate opportunity;
- changed local trust in the player;
- new persistent contacts;
- future debt, loyalty, resentment, or rivalry states.

### Required existing systems

- mission conditions and completion effects;
- government/reputation data;
- trade commodities and system prices;
- existing human systems, planets, fleets, jobs, conversations, and news content.

### Required future systems

- selectable/derived RPG backgrounds;

- exposed dynamic economy/world-state values;

No engine change is required for static mission versions. A generalized background-selection UI and simulation-query conditions would require future builder work.
