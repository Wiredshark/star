# Narrative Diversity Policy

## Purpose

Dynamic narrative must demonstrate breadth across the simulated world. Freight, cargo loss, logistics, market shortages, and convoy/security response are useful systems, but they are only one narrative domain and must not become the default premise for new story content.

## Required domain diversity

Story work should draw from multiple independent domains, including:

- faction politics, elections, leadership disputes, diplomacy, and reputation;
- personal relationships, rivalries, debts, betrayals, family ties, and companions;
- exploration, archaeology, anomalies, scientific discoveries, and expeditions;
- crime, smuggling, bounty hunting, investigation, policing, and corruption;
- wars, territorial changes, occupations, ceasefires, and military command;
- religious, ideological, cultural, and civic conflicts;
- corporate disputes, labor conflicts, espionage, sabotage, and industrial accidents;
- disasters, disease, migration, refugees, infrastructure failure, and ecological stress;
- ownership, inheritance, property, local law, and contested claims;
- mysteries driven by NPC schedules, evidence, locations, ownership, or contradictory testimony;
- economy and freight, when they genuinely fit the region and story rather than serving as a default template.

## Anti-repetition rule

A new story packet must not use freight, cargo loss, shortages, convoy escort, trade-route security, or market disruption as its primary conflict if either of the two most recently authored or implemented story packets already used one of those as a primary conflict.

More generally, no single narrative domain should account for more than one third of the active implementation-ready story backlog unless a regional arc explicitly requires that concentration and the handoff documents why.

## Systemic-input requirement

Every implementation-ready story packet must declare at least two meaningful world-state inputs. At least one input should come from outside the economy/freight domain unless the story is explicitly an economic story.

Useful inputs include faction control, war state, crime pressure, population movement, character relationships, player reputation, planetary conditions, discoveries, ownership state, law/enforcement posture, local governance, environmental hazards, and prior player choices.

## Consequence diversity

Outcomes should not all resolve into money, cargo availability, or security-fleet intensity. Prefer consequences that can alter combinations of:

- persistent character relationships;
- faction reputation or control;
- access to locations, services, or information;
- legal status or criminal attention;
- future dialogue and mission availability;
- NPC schedules, roles, loyalties, or survival;
- war/diplomatic state;
- local governance or ownership;
- discoveries and knowledge available to the player;
- migration/population or settlement conditions;
- economy/logistics where relevant.

## Per-round story-agent check

Before handing off a story round, the author must include a short `DIVERSITY_CHECK` section stating:

1. the primary narrative domain of each new packet;
2. the two previous packet domains considered;
3. which non-economic world-state inputs are used;
4. why the new content is not a reskin of an existing freight/logistics crisis;
5. which persistent consequence types the player can create.

If those answers show repeated premise structure, revise the packet before implementation handoff.

## Design target

The project should feel like a living RPG/immersive simulation in which logistics is one pressure among politics, relationships, crime, war, exploration, culture, law, ownership, environment, and discovery. Dynamic narrative quality is measured by how many different parts of the simulated world can meaningfully create and remember stories, not by how many variants can be produced from one freight system.
