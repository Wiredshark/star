mission "Quarg History: Peaceful Port Register"
	minor
	invisible
	source
		government "Quarg"
	to offer
		has "First Contact: Quarg: offered"
		random < 4
	on offer
		conversation
			`An information alcove lists old port rules in several languages beside modern translations. The oldest entries are terse: visitors are expected to trade honestly, settle disputes without violence, and leave weapons secured aboard their ships. Later entries add procedures for arbitration, damaged cargo, rescue claims, and the protection of travelers whose governments are hostile to one another.`
			`	A note says the rules were never meant to make Quarg ports politically neutral in every conflict. Their purpose was narrower and more practical: anyone who arrived in peace should be able to depart in peace, and commerce should not become an excuse to carry distant wars onto Quarg worlds.`
				decline


mission "Quarg History: Protected Community Ledger"
	minor
	invisible
	source
		government "Quarg"
	to offer
		has "First Contact: Quarg: offered"
		random < 4
	on offer
		conversation
			`A quiet memorial wall contains no heroic portraits, only lists of settlements, transport routes, supply allotments, and population counts. The translation explains that the Quarg have repeatedly sheltered communities whose survival depended on separating them from an ongoing conflict. The records focus less on battles than on the difficult work afterward: food, habitat space, medical care, translation, local administration, and deciding how much authority the protected community should retain.`
			`	One annotation warns that protection can preserve a people without resolving the conflict that endangered them. The archive therefore treats refuge as a continuing obligation rather than a single act of rescue.`
				decline


mission "Quarg History: Path Stewardship Record"
	minor
	invisible
	source
		government "Quarg"
	to offer
		has "First Contact: Quarg: offered"
		random < 4
	on offer
		conversation
			`A navigational exhibit maps ordinary hyperlanes beside broad blank regions where no public route is shown. The text says that the Quarg have traveled for millions of years and have learned to think of access between regions as something that carries responsibilities as well as opportunities. Some routes are surveyed and shared freely; others are watched closely because contact can expose a weaker society to dangers it is not prepared to face.`
			`	The display carefully avoids describing the Quarg's own means of traveling beyond the ordinary pathways. It does, however, repeat a principle familiar from their conversations about the Drak: the ability to reach somewhere does not by itself create the right to open that path to everyone.`
				decline
