			`A natural-history archive has begun preserving the earliest shipboard records from the Umbral Reach that describe the creatures now catalogued as Iije. The first useful entry is notably cautious: scanners initially classified the small organism in L-6181 as little more than a collection of matter, while the crew's direct observation recorded unmistakable movement and behavior.`
			`\tAn archivist says that contradiction became part of the record rather than something to be edited away. mission "Iije History: Growth Record"
	minor
	invisible
	source "Midgard"
	to offer
		has "Rulei: Umbral Reach: offered"
		random < 4
	on offer
		conversation
			`A xenobiology office displays comparison sheets for the small Jje and the much larger Ayym encountered deeper in the Umbral Reach. Current field classification treats them as adolescent and adult forms of the same broader Iije life cycle, but the annotations are careful about how much of that cycle has actually been witnessed.`
			`\tThe smaller form is associated with consumption of stellar dust, matter, and energy and is often drawn toward light. The larger form can survive on a far greater scale and appears capable of gathering matter over immense spans of time. Those observations fit a growth sequence, but no human expedition has watched one organism transform from one form into the other.`
			`\tResearchers therefore preserve both the working model and its evidentiary limits. A useful natural history can accumulate over generations without turning a plausible life-cycle reconstruction into an event someone falsely claims to have observed.`
				decline


mission "Iije History: Grouping and Transport Archive"
	minor
	invisible
	source "Mirrorlake"
	to offer
		has "Rulei: Umbral Reach: offered"
		random < 4
	on offer
		conversation
			`A behavioral archive contains route sketches from captains who encountered groups of Jje and the rarer Ayym. Several independent reports describe the larger creatures carrying smaller Jje through the Reach, sometimes more than one at a time, but the archive refuses to label the behavior as parenting, migration, protection, or feeding.`
			`\tA curator explains that this restraint is deliberate. Repeated transport behavior is an observation; its social purpose is an interpretation. The Iije are mute to human instruments and have never supplied a convenient explanation of their own behavior.`
			`\tInstead, each sighting records group size, stellar conditions, relative motion, nearby light sources, and whether the smaller creatures remained with the adult afterward. Over time, the institution hopes patterns will narrow the possibilities without erasing the uncertainty that made the original encounters scientifically valuable.`
				decline


mission "Iije History: Stellar Feeding Survey"
	minor
	invisible
	source "Earth"
	to offer
		has "Rulei: Umbral Reach: offered"
		random < 4
	on offer
		conversation
			`A survey office has assembled a sparse map of Iije sightings against stellar brightness, dust concentration, and local debris fields. The records support the long-standing observation that the smaller creatures gather stellar matter and energy and are often attracted to light, while the much larger form appears adapted to collecting material on a scale no short expedition can meaningfully follow.`
			`\tThe practical notes are almost as important as the biology. Bright work lights can change the movement of nearby Jje, and a creature harmless at a distance may still cross a ship's path if a crew mistakes attraction for deliberate approach.`
			`\tThe archive therefore treats navigation practice and natural history as parts of the same record: observe without provoking, note the conditions that may have influenced the encounter, and do not convert an animal's response to energy or light into evidence of hostility, curiosity, or intent that the observation itself cannot support.`
				decline
