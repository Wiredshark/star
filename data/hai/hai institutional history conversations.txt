			`A quiet civic archive displays maintenance ledgers for buildings, transit systems, irrigation works, and public machinery whose service lives span many generations. The oldest entries are sparse and idiosyncratic. Later records use increasingly consistent inspections, replacement schedules, and handoff notes, with each steward recording what the next generation will need to know.`
			`	A Hai archivist explains that the apparent permanence of their cities is less a matter of structures that never wear out than of institutions that expect care to continue long after any one worker retires. 			`A regional agricultural museum has devoted one room to old harvest-allocation records. The documents track reserves, transport capacity, crop failures, and shipments between worlds, but one category repeatedly appears without being treated as ordinary trade: food and other necessities sent northward despite recurring raids and political hostility.`
			`	The accompanying commentary says that the policy was argued over many times, especially after costly attacks. Supporters insisted that defensive force and humanitarian provision answered different questions: communities could repel raids without deciding that civilians beyond the border should be left without the necessities of life. What began as emergency shipments eventually accumulated procedures for reserves, routing, and accountability that survived changes in local leadership.`

mission "Hai Quiet Border Archive"
	minor
	source
		government "Hai"
		not attributes "uninhabited"
	"offer precedence" 5
	to offer
		random < 1
	on offer
		conversation
			`A historical display near a port security office contains warning beacons, patrol schedules, civilian evacuation notices, and old maps of the northern approaches. The exhibit is striking for how rarely the documents describe offensive objectives. Most concern detection, interception, protected routes, rescue responsibilities, and the difficult task of keeping ordinary traffic moving while raids remained possible.`
			`	A note from the curators argues that the border's long stability came from making restraint procedural rather than merely aspirational. Patrol officers were given narrow defensive duties, merchants learned standardized warning practices, and civil authorities maintained relief and rescue arrangements separately from military responses. The resulting system did not end conflict, but it made escalation less dependent on the anger or fear of whoever happened to respond first.`
				decline
