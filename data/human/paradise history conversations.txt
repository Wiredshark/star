The earliest programs described are small family endowments meant to educate relatives, clients, and promising employees; later entries show universities combining hundreds of such funds into larger institutions with formal admissions boards and public charitable status.`
			`\tOne caption points out that the expansion of scholarships never erased the influence of their founders. 			`\tThe modern photographs show students from dozens of worlds standing beneath donor names carved into buildings. The display presents this as one of the enduring contradictions of Paradise education: institutions that can widen opportunity while remaining visibly shaped by concentrated private wealth.`

	"offer precedence" 5
	to offer
		random < 1
	on offer

			`Near a transit stop you find a small municipal museum built around an old maintenance depot. Its exhibits trace how household staff, gardeners, cleaners, mechanics, drivers, cooks, and security workers once moved through a patchwork of private service roads and employer-owned housing before growing cities required shared transit, utilities, schools, and emergency services.`
			`\tThe curators emphasize that these changes were rarely planned all at once. Employers wanted reliable access to labor, workers wanted neighborhoods not wholly controlled by their jobs, and local governments wanted taxes and infrastructure that could function across estate boundaries. The resulting service districts became ordinary municipalities even while many residents continued to work in the wealthy enclaves around them.`
			`\tOutside, a stream of uniformed workers boards a public shuttle toward the hills. The old depot makes the route look less like a convenience than the latest version of a social arrangement that has been renegotiated for generations.`
				decline

			`A gallery adjoining the spaceport is advertising a retrospective on the Paradise worlds' long tradition of charitable expeditions. Old posters show medical drives, clothing collections, food shipments, school funds, and disaster appeals organized by clubs, churches, universities, corporations, and prominent families.`
			`\tThe accompanying history notes that these campaigns did real work, but also became part of elite social life. Successful organizers gained prestige, donors competed to sponsor visible projects, and entire institutions grew around deciding which distant communities were worthy of assistance. Critics periodically objected that the people receiving aid had little influence over what was collected or how they were portrayed.`
			`\tThe final panel describes modern charities as more professional and more closely audited, yet still dependent on the same mixture of compassion, status, tax policy, and public reputation. You think of the polished donation appeals that are still common in Paradise spaceports and wonder how much has changed besides the presentation.`
				decline
