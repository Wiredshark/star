	to offer
		random < 1
	on offer
		conversation
			`One wall of the terminal archive is covered by old cargo labels, transfer receipts, and photographs of sealed research crates. The accompanying text explains that Deep laboratories often depended on materials gathered far outside the region, and that scientific value could be lost if the history of a sample became uncertain.`
			`	Over time, laboratories, freight crews, and Deep Security began preserving a common chain of custody: where a shipment originated, which seals had been broken, what storage conditions it experienced, which substitutions were made, and who accepted responsibility at each transfer.`
			`	The archive emphasizes that a signed receipt was never treated as proof that the contents were what a researcher hoped they were. Custody records preserved the path of the evidence; verification still belonged to the laboratory.`

		not attributes "station"
	"offer precedence" 5
	to offer
		random < 1
	on offer
		conversation
			`The local history exhibit includes a series of old route advisories. Some list pirate sightings, others report missing ships, damaged beacons, or warnings copied from neighboring ports. Beside each warning is a column identifying who reported it and whether later traffic confirmed the danger.`
			`	The curator explains that Deep convoy planners gradually learned to separate a route warning from the reason for believing it. A report from a surviving crew, three repeated copies of the same rumor, and a pattern inferred from disappearances could all justify caution, but they were not the same kind of evidence.`
			`	The archive kept both accurate warnings and embarrassing false alarms. Removing the latter, the curator says, would make the historical record look more certain than the people making the decisions ever were.`
				decline



mission "Deep Experimental Supply Standards Archive"
	minor
	source
		government "Republic"
		attributes "deep"
		not attributes "station"
	"offer precedence" 5
	to offer
		random < 1
	on offer
		conversation
			`A compact museum display shows several generations of laboratory shipping containers: insulated boxes, vibration-isolated racks, radiation shielding, pressure vessels, and ordinary freight crates covered with increasingly specific handling marks.`
			`	An accompanying timeline describes how Deep research centers, manufacturers, and carriers developed shared descriptions for storage limits, contamination risk, orientation, temperature, shock exposure, and replacement materials. The standards did not make every experiment compatible, but they reduced the number of failures caused by the shipment itself.`
			`	One surviving committee note warns against confusing compatibility with equivalence. A substitute part or material might fit the same container and survive the same journey while still changing the experiment. The archive presents that warning as one of the more durable lessons of the Deep's research economy.`
				decline
