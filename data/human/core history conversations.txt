The earliest records differ wildly from world to world: some list only an owner and a hull name, while others include cargo limits, crew obligations, liens, inspection notes, and handwritten amendments accumulated over decades.`
			`	A curator explains that the dense paperwork of modern Core ports emerged gradually as local registries learned to recognize one another. 			`A small industrial museum has arranged generations of couplings, pressure fittings, power connectors, cargo locks, and access panels along one wall. The oldest examples are almost all incompatible with each other. The newer the display becomes, the more the parts begin to share familiar dimensions.`
			`	The exhibit notes that Core shipbuilding did not become dominant through a single breakthrough. Manufacturers, insurers, ports, and repair yards spent generations arguing over standards that let components, tools, and trained labor move between competing shipyards. The resulting compromises were rarely elegant, but they made repairs and large-scale production far more predictable.`
