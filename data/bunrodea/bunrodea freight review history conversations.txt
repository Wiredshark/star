			`\tThe surrounding history explains that this practice emerged after overloaded clerks discovered that age alone was a poor measure of suspicion. A petition could remain open because of a missing seal, a damaged translation, an absent estate factor, a duplicated inspection request, or a genuine conflict over authority. Without the reason, later offices tended to treat delay itself as evidence that something must be wrong.`
			`\tModern queue displays still preserve those categories. The exhibit's final caption says the goal was never to make every petition fast. It was to make waiting legible enough that an old delay would not silently acquire a new meaning every time the file changed hands.`
