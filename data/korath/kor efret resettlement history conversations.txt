mission "Kor Efret History: Passage Contribution Ledger"
	minor
	invisible

		attributes "efret"
		not attributes "station"
	to offer
		random < 4
	on offer
		conversation
			`A shipping office is displaying old passenger accounts beside diagrams of small freighters. Many payments are strikingly uneven: one family contributes cargo, another a few credits, another workshop time or replacement parts. Several journeys appear to have cost far more than the passengers themselves could provide.`
			`\tThe records suggest that low-fare passage became practical through shared contributions rather than a single subsidy. Workshops, relatives, traders, and sympathetic carriers each covered part of a trip, especially when a family needed to reach a safer settlement or reunite with relatives already living under Quarg protection.`
			`\tThe ledger preserves the imbalance instead of pretending every journey was an ordinary commercial fare. Transport could be charity, debt, reciprocal help, or paid work all at once, and the exact obligation depended on who had enough capacity to help at that moment.`
				decline

			`\tA nearby display shows Kor Efreti, Quarg, and visiting crews repeating the same gestures and phrases until everyone agrees on what was meant. Over time, recurring translations appear to have been preserved as practical precedents rather than treated as perfect dictionaries.`
			`\tThe archive is careful about ambiguity. A successful translation is recorded with the situation in which it worked, because a phrase that settled a passenger request or family reunion once may not carry exactly the same meaning in a different dispute. Mediation became durable by remembering context, not by pretending imperfect translation had disappeared.`

mission "Kor Efret History: Return and Resettlement Ledger"
	minor
	invisible
	source
		attributes "efret"
		not attributes "station"
	to offer
		random < 4
	on offer
		conversation
			`A wall display compares damaged districts with lists of households that left, returned, remained elsewhere, or have not made a final decision. Repair status appears beside air-quality marks, workshop capacity, food production, transport availability, and symbols for family members living on other worlds.`
			`\tThe pattern makes one thing obvious: rebuilding a place and resettling it are not the same task. A building can be habitable while employment is missing; a workshop can reopen while a family chooses to stay near relatives elsewhere; a transport route can return while environmental damage still makes permanent return uncertain.`
			`\tThe ledger treats those outcomes separately. Recovery is measured not by forcing every displaced Kor Efret back to an old address, but by keeping a truthful record of what has become possible, what remains unsafe or impractical, and which people have chosen a different future.`
				decline
