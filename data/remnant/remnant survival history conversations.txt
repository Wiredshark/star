	minor
	source
		government "Remnant"
	"offer precedence" 5
	to offer
		random < 1
	on offer
		conversation
			`A quiet archive near the spaceport preserves copies of household records, technical manuals, song notation, and early settlement inventories. The oldest material is uneven: some pages are meticulous, while others are fragments copied from damaged storage or reconstructed from several families' memories.`
			`\tA signed explanation says that isolation made continuity an institution rather than an assumption. The first settlers had fled the Alpha Wars with whatever records they could carry, and later generations repeatedly recopied medical histories, engineering knowledge, family links, and pieces of the ancestral culture so that a single accident or failed archive would not erase them.`

	to offer
		random < 1

			`A technical gallery contains rows of alien components mounted beside Remnant-made replacements. Some artifacts are recognizable as Korath or other non-human technology; others have been rebuilt so many times that only a few original pieces remain.`
			`\tAn archivist explains in sign that recovered technology was never treated as a single discovery. Salvage crews, engineers, and defense units developed records for where a component came from, how it had been damaged, which interfaces were understood, and what modifications had been made before it entered ordinary service. Over generations, that habit turned scavenged hardware into a body of repeatable engineering knowledge rather than a collection of irreplaceable curiosities.`

	minor
	source
		government "Remnant"
	"offer precedence" 5
	to offer
		random < 1
	on offer
		conversation
			`A civic display shows old reserve ledgers listing food, medicine, structural materials, spare machinery, transport capacity, and emergency berths. The entries are grouped not only by settlement, but by how long each stockpile could sustain neighboring communities if normal traffic were interrupted.`
			`\tThe display explains that the Remnant's isolation made shortages a collective security problem. Local stores remained locally managed, but settlements gradually adopted common reporting and transfer practices so that patrol losses, damaged transports, or temporary route closures would not force every world to discover too late what the others could spare. The ledgers became as much a map of mutual obligation as an inventory.`
				decline
