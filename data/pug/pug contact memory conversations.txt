			`	One curator explains that this practice grew out of an uncomfortable fact: the occupation was undeniable, but many ordinary accounts did not fit the usual expectations of a conquering army. Early inspection teams mapped unfamiliar power paths, sealed systems they could not identify, and marked components that could be maintained safely with human tools without pretending to understand the whole design.`
			`	The register eventually became a shared reference for port engineers, utility crews, insurers, and emergency services. Its rules favor reversible repairs and meticulous records whenever Pug construction is involved. The exhibit notes that preserving a working city built by another species required a different habit from either salvaging alien technology or replacing it outright: sometimes the safest form of understanding was knowing exactly what had not yet been understood.`

			`A small research room contains hundreds of recorded interviews about encounters with the Pug: dock workers, pilots, soldiers, translators, medics, and civilians all describing behavior that they interpreted in different ways. The catalog deliberately preserves contradictions instead of smoothing them into a single official narrative.`
			`	An introductory note explains why. Even after direct contact, the Pug have often remained difficult or impossible for humans to question, and encounters elsewhere have shown that a language barrier can make apparent silence look like deliberate secrecy. The archive therefore distinguishes observed actions from guessed motives, giving later diplomats and historians a record of uncertainty rather than a false certainty inherited from the first witnesses.`

			`A port-history display charts traffic through Deneb after the Pug were gone. Ship counts rose quickly as merchants rediscovered the system's value as a route between several human regions. Old landing schedules show long queues, improvised holding patterns, and temporary cargo yards appearing faster than permanent port facilities could be expanded.`

What began as a practical response to a strange postwar bottleneck became part of the port's normal operating memory.
