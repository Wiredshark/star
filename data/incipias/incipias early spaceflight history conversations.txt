mission "Incipias History: Mining Survey Register"
	minor
	invisible
	source
		government "Conlatio"
	to offer
		random < 4
	on offer
		conversation
			`Several miners are arguing over a cluster of three-dimensional maps filled with colored points and looping paths. Each point appears to carry a history: who surveyed it, what was extracted there, which hazards were encountered, and whether another expedition considered the site exhausted.`
			`\tThe register seems to have grown alongside the Incipias mining fleets. Once enough civilian crews began leaving the home system, simply remembering which rocks had already been examined was no longer sufficient. Survey records became a way to avoid wasted voyages, conflicting claims, and repeated exposure to the same dangerous regions.`
			`\tFor a species still early in its expansion beyond home, the maps function almost like a collective memory of exploration. Every mundane extraction job leaves behind information that makes the next journey slightly less uncertain.`
				decline

			`In a crowded civic building, an Incipias technician studies a public index of research projects. Most entries are ordinary improvements to propulsion, shields, energy storage, or industrial equipment, but a handful contain little more than a project code and a notice that technical details remain restricted.`
			`\tThe technician complains that major breakthroughs have become rare while government laboratories promise work that ordinary citizens cannot inspect. Another Incipias nearby responds by pointing to older entries where once-restricted projects were eventually published, standardized, and adopted by civilian yards.`
			`\tThe disagreement suggests an institution still learning how to balance experimentation, safety, military secrecy, and public trust. Their scientific history is not only a list of inventions, but a recurring argument over when new knowledge stops belonging to the laboratory that discovered it and becomes infrastructure for everyone else.`
